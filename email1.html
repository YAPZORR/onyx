The Checklist

  1. 1.Is the batch number on the vial?

    Without it, nothing connects what arrived to what was tested.

  2. 2.Is the testing lab named?

    A lab you can look up on its own, not a factory document that shipped with the box.

  3. 3.Is purity a real result or a round number?

    Ours read 99.957%, 99.320%, 99.136%. Round figures are the tell.

  4. 4.Was identity confirmed?

    Purity says how much of the sample is one compound. LC-MS says which compound.

  5. 5.Was net content measured?

    Weighed against the label, published per batch.

  6. 6.Is the full panel there?

    Endotoxin, heavy metals across 4 analytes, and conformity tested vial by vial.

Step 1

Find the batch number on the label.

It looks like GHK0925-100-1. Compound, size, lot.

Step 2

Search it on the Lab Results page.

The search bar takes a compound name, a batch number or a verification code.

Step 3

Open the report and read 4 fields.

Batch, purity, net content, identity confirmation.

Worked Example

Batch GHK0925-100-1. GHK-Cu, 100mg.

Purity:99.978%

Measured net content:109.58mg against a 100mg label

Endotoxin:tested against a 0.5 EU/mL limit

Heavy metals:arsenic, cadmium, lead and mercury, all negative

Conformity:each sampled vial broken out separately

Why It Matters

A certificate with no batch number is a document about some material, somewhere. The batch number is what makes it yours.
